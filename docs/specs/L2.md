# L2 - Detailed Requirements

Each requirement traces to one requirement in [L1.md](./L1.md). Acceptance criteria are written
Given / When / Then so each becomes a test. An acceptance test names the requirements it covers
in a header comment:

```typescript
// Acceptance Test
// Traces to: L2-001, L2-003
// Description: A person joins a team from its invite link with a postal code
```

Names, cities, postal codes and dates in the criteria are examples from the prototype's sample
team (Northwind, 13 teammates in 12 time zones) as the mocks show it at their "now", Monday 27
October 2025, 14:05 UTC, and from the prototype's seeded team of 500; they are the cast, not
rules. "The viewer" is the teammate looking at the screen. Words in quotation marks are the
mocks' own, character for character.

Where a requirement says something is **not designed yet**, the mocks do not draw it; it is not
built before its design exists, and nothing is invented in its place.

## L2-001: Open an invite link
**Traces to:** L1-001

An invite link opens the Join screen for its team. The screen shows the team's name, how many
teammates are already on the globe, the initials of up to five of them, and the globe with their
pins, before the person has given anything. Under the form, "Already on Mondino? Sign in" leads
to Sign In (L2-052).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a valid invite link for the team Northwind with 12 teammates, when a person opens it, then the Join screen shows "Put yourself on the map.", "Joining Northwind · 12 already here" beside the initials of up to five of its teammates, and a globe with 12 pins.
2. Given a valid invite link, when the Join screen is shown before joining, then it shows of the teammates only a bare pin for each, in the pin colour and with no name, city, local time or weather, and the initials and colour of up to five of them, the five who joined most recently; the initials are not tied to any pin, and the response carries no teammate's name, id or city.
3. Given an invite link that does not exist, was mistyped, has been withdrawn or has been reset (L2-055), when a person opens it, then a notice stands in place of the form: "This invite link no longer works", "Someone on the team reset it, so it can’t be used to join. Ask whoever sent it for the new link." and "Start your own team", which leads to Create Team (L2-053). The notice is the same in each of these cases, and the screen shows no team name, no count and no pins.
4. Given a person who is already a teammate of the team on this device, when they open the team's invite link, then they are taken to the Globe screen and no second pin is made.
5. Given an invite link is being opened, when the answer has not arrived, then the Join screen shows "Put yourself on the map." with placeholders for the team and the form and a globe with no pins, and announces "Opening your invite link…".
6. Given the backend does not answer, when a person opens an invite link, then a notice stands in place of the form: "We couldn’t open this invite", "Mondino didn’t answer. Check your connection and try the link again — it hasn’t been used up." and "Try again"; the screen shows no team name, no count and no pins, and does not say the invite link no longer works.
7. Given one client has opened invite links too often and the open is refused with 429 (L2-045), when a person opens an invite link, then the screen shows the same state as when the backend does not answer: "We couldn’t open this invite", with "Try again", and no team name, no count and no pins.
8. Given the Join screen in any of its states, when it is shown, then "Already on Mondino? Sign in" is under the form or the notice, and "Sign in" leads to Sign In.

## L2-002: Give a name, a country and a postal code
**Traces to:** L1-001

The Join form has exactly three fields: the teammate's name, a country chosen from the supported
countries, and a postal code. It asks for no street address, email address or phone number.
(While the postal code lookup is down, a fourth field, "Your city", stands in for the postal
code: L2-004.)

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the Join screen, when it is shown, then the form has the fields "Your name", "Country" and "Postal code" and no other field, under the line "Your pin shows teammates your local time, weather and what’s happening where you are."
2. Given the Country field, when it is opened, then it lists the supported countries in alphabetical order; to begin with these are the thirteen: Australia, Brazil, Canada, Germany, Ghana, India, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Mexico, Sweden, United Arab Emirates and United States.
3. Given a name that is empty or only spaces, when the form is submitted, then the pin is not placed, the name field is marked invalid (`aria-invalid`) and a message under it says "Add your name so teammates know whose pin this is."
4. Given a name longer than 60 characters, when the form is submitted, then the pin is not placed and the message under the name field gives the limit.
5. Given the Join screen with nothing typed, when it is shown, then the text "Only your city is shown. Your postal code stays private." is visible under the postal code field.
6. Given an empty postal code, when the form is submitted, then no lookup is made, the postal code field is marked invalid (`aria-invalid`) and a message under it says "Enter a postal code."
7. Given a form submitted with a name or a postal code missing, when the messages are shown, then a summary above the fields says what is missing ("Two things are missing"), links to each field ("Add your name", "Enter a postal code"), and takes focus.
8. Given the Join screen, when it is shown, then it names no outside source; the line under the form is "Already on Mondino? Sign in" (L2-001).

## L2-003: Look up the postal code
**Traces to:** L1-001

On Join, the postal code is looked up for the chosen country when the teammate leaves the field
or submits the form. While it is looked up the screen says so; when it is found the screen names
the city and the weather there, places the teammate's pin on the globe, and the button changes
from "Place my pin" to "Join" and the team's name. Create Team looks its postal code up in the
same way (L2-053); Profile & Settings starts the lookup from its "Look up" button (L2-032).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Italy and the postal code "20121", when the teammate leaves the postal code field, then the screen shows "Finding 20121…" and the button reads "Placing your pin…" and is disabled until the lookup answers.
2. Given the lookup finds Milan, when it answers, then the screen shows the city and region ("Milan, Lombardy"), that the pin is placed at city level ("pinned at city level"), and the current temperature and weather there ("13°", "and foggy right now.").
3. Given the lookup has found the city, when the screen updates, then the globe turns to that city and holds, a new pin labelled "You · Milan" appears there and pulses once (not under reduced motion), and the button reads "Join Northwind".
4. Given a found postal code, when the teammate changes the postal code or the country, then the found city and the new pin are removed and the button reads "Place my pin" again.
5. Given Canada and the postal code "L5B 3C1", when it is looked up, then the city found is Mississauga; and given "M5V 2T6", then the city found is Toronto.
6. Given a postal code typed with or without its usual space or hyphen and in upper or lower case ("l5b3c1", "L5B 3C1"), when it is looked up, then both find the same city.

## L2-004: A postal code that cannot be found, and a lookup that is down
**Traces to:** L1-001

A postal code that does not match the country's format, or that the lookup does not know, is
refused with a message that names the postal code, the country and the form that country's
postal codes take. Nothing is placed. When the lookup itself is down, the person picks their
city instead, so nobody is kept from joining because an outside source failed. The same holds
on Create Team (L2-053).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Italy and the postal code "2012", when it is looked up, then an alert says "We couldn’t find “2012” in Italy. Italian postal codes have five digits, like 20121."
2. Given Canada and a postal code that does not fit the pattern letter-digit-letter digit-letter-digit, when it is looked up, then an alert gives the Canadian form with an example ("Canadian postal codes look like A1A 1A1."), and no request is sent to the postal code source.
3. Given a postal code in the right form that the source does not know, when it is looked up, then the same alert is shown and no pin is placed.
4. Given an alert is shown, when the teammate edits the postal code, then the alert is removed.
5. Given the postal code source does not answer in time, when the lookup fails on Join, then the postal code field is switched off with what was typed kept in it, and a field "Your city" appears with the text "Postal code lookup is down right now, so pick your city instead. Your pin goes to its centre.", announced to assistive technology; the screen does not say the postal code is wrong.
6. Given the field "Your city", when the person types in it, then it lists the cities of the chosen country that match what was typed, as a list operable by keyboard and by touch; the list comes from the backend (L2-044), and no outside source is called from the browser.
7. Given the person chooses Milan from the list, when it is chosen, then the button reads "Place my pin in Milan", and using it places the pin at the city, as a postal code would (L2-006), after which joining goes on as in L2-005.
8. Given the lookup was down when the Join screen was last shown, when the screen is next opened, then the lookup is tried again and, if it answers, the postal code field is on and "Your city" is absent.

## L2-005: Join the team
**Traces to:** L1-001

Once the city is found, joining adds the teammate to the team with a pin at that city, and takes
them to the Globe screen with their own pin selected. A team holds up to 500 teammates.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a name and a found postal code, when the teammate chooses "Join Northwind", then the team has one more teammate, and the Globe screen opens with the new teammate's pin on it.
2. Given a teammate who has just joined, when the Globe screen first shows their pin, then the pin pulses once to mark it as new (and does not pulse under reduced motion), and a toast says "You’re on the map." and "Northwind can now see your pin in Milan.", is announced once to assistive technology, and dismisses itself after 4 seconds.
3. Given the join request fails, when the failure is reported, then the teammate stays on the Join screen with what they typed intact, an alert says "We couldn’t add you to Northwind." and "Your details are still here — try joining again.", and the button reads "Try joining again".
4. Given a teammate double-clicks "Join", when both requests arrive, then exactly one pin is made.
5. Given a team that has reached 500 teammates, when a person opens its invite link, then a notice stands in place of the form: "Northwind is full" and "The team has all 500 of its places taken. Ask a teammate at Northwind to tell you when a place opens, then open this link again."; the team's name is shown because the link is good, and nothing else about the team is: no count, no initials and a globe with no pins.
6. Given a team that fills while a person is on the Join form, when their join is refused with the code `team_full`, then the same notice takes the place of the form and no pin is made.
7. Given the teammate has chosen "Join Northwind", when the join request has not been answered, then the button reads "Joining Northwind…" and is disabled, and the name, country and postal code cannot be changed.
8. Given a join request refused with 429, when it is shown, then the Join screen shows the countdown of L2-045 and what was typed is kept.

## L2-006: A pin is placed at the city
**Traces to:** L1-002

A postal code is resolved to its city: the municipality it lies in, the level at which
Mississauga and Toronto are different places. The pin takes the city's own position, never the
position of the postal code.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given two teammates in Toronto with different postal codes, when their pins are read from the backend, then both carry the same position, the position of Toronto.
2. Given a teammate in Mississauga and one in Toronto, when the globe is shown, then their pins are at different positions and each is labelled with its own city.
3. Given any teammate, when their pin's position is compared with the position of their postal code, then the pin's position is the city's and does not change when the postal code changes within the same city.
4. Given a postal code in a rural area that belongs to no city, when it is looked up, then the pin is placed at the city Mondino holds nearest the place the source gives, at that city's own position, and that city's name is shown as the city.

## L2-007: A postal code is never shown to another teammate
**Traces to:** L1-002

A postal code is returned only to the teammate it belongs to, on their own Join and Profile &
Settings screens. Everything another teammate receives carries the city and country only.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a teammate reads their team, when the response is inspected, then no other teammate's postal code, nor any part or hash of it, is present in it.
2. Given a teammate's panel on the Globe screen, a row on the Team screen or a moment on the Moments screen, when another teammate looks at it, then it shows the city (the panel and the Team row with its country) and no postal code.
3. Given a teammate reads their own profile, when the response is inspected, then it carries their own postal code and no one else's.
4. Given any screen, when the browser's storage (local, session, IndexedDB, cookies) and the address bar are inspected, then no postal code is present in them.
5. Given a request for another teammate's profile, when it is made, then it is refused with 403 or 404 and returns no postal code.

## L2-008: The globe is three-dimensional and drawn from Blender-built assets
**Traces to:** L1-003

The globe is a lit, shaded 3D model: land raised from the sea, soft shadows, rounded forms and
saturated colour, in the look of a modern animated film. It has colours of its own, as the mocks
draw it: a bright blue ocean with a deeper rim, green land with sand and forest, white ice and
cloud, a dusk-shaded night side and an ink outline. The brand's pin and sun mark the people and
moments on it. Its model and textures are made in Blender, exported to a web format and
bundled with the app.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the Globe screen on a device with WebGL, when the globe is drawn, then it is rendered in 3D with perspective and lighting, and continents are recognisable.
2. Given the globe's assets, when the app is built, then they are served from the app's own origin, and the browser makes no request to any other origin to draw the globe.
3. Given a device without WebGL, or where the globe's assets fail to load, when the Globe screen opens, then a still picture of the globe is shown in its place with every pin in its place: pins lift and open their panels as usual (L2-012, L2-023), and the picture does not turn and is not dragged. The legend says "Still view — 3D isn’t available in this browser" in place of "Drag to spin", and under 640 px a line under the globe says "Still view — 3D isn’t available in this browser. Pins still open."
4. Given a low-power device (see L2-046), when the globe is drawn, then it uses a lower level of detail and keeps its look: lighting, colour and pins are unchanged.
5. Given a first-time visitor, when the Globe screen opens, then a placeholder in the globe's place is shown until the 3D globe is ready, and the layout does not shift when it appears.

## L2-009: The globe turns, spins and holds
**Traces to:** L1-003

Left alone, the globe turns slowly on its own. It is dragged to spin, by pointer or touch, and
turned with the arrow keys. When a pin is selected the globe turns to bring it to the front and
holds there.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the Globe screen with no pin selected and no interaction, when 5 seconds pass, then the globe has turned eastward on its own.
2. Given the globe, when the teammate drags across it by mouse or by touch, then the globe spins with the drag and stops turning on its own until 5 seconds after the drag ends.
3. Given a pin is selected, when the selection is made, then the globe turns so the pin faces the viewer within 1 second and stays there until the selection is cleared.
4. Given the globe has keyboard focus, when an arrow key is pressed, then the globe turns in that direction by a fixed step.
5. Given reduced motion is requested, when the Globe screen is shown, then the globe does not turn on its own, and turning to a selected pin happens without animation.
6. Given the browser tab is hidden, when it stays hidden, then the globe neither turns nor draws, and it resumes when the tab is shown again.
7. Given a vertical drag that begins on the globe on a phone, when the globe cannot turn further in that direction, then the page scrolls, so the globe never traps the page.

## L2-010: The night side is shaded
**Traces to:** L1-003

The half of the globe where it is night right now is shaded, from the sun's real position at the
current time, and the shading moves as time passes.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the clock says 12:00 UTC on 21 March, when the globe is drawn, then a point at longitude 0° on the equator is lit and a point at longitude 180° is shaded.
2. Given a teammate whose local time is 02:00, when the globe is turned to their pin, then their pin sits in the shaded half.
3. Given the Globe screen stays open for an hour, when the hour has passed, then the edge of the shading has moved westward by about 15° of longitude.
4. Given a pin on the night side, when it is shown, then the pin and its label remain as readable as on the day side (contrast per L2-041).
5. Given a width of 640 px or more, when the Globe screen is shown, then a legend names "Night side", "Moment this week", "Teammates close together" (beside a sample group pin, L2-013) and "Drag to spin"; it leaves out "Moment this week" when the viewer has turned off every kind (L2-022), and says why the globe is still in place of "Drag to spin" on the still picture (L2-008).

## L2-011: A pin shows a teammate
**Traces to:** L1-004

A pin shows the teammate's initials in the teammate's own colour, and a label beside it with
their local time, the weather and the temperature. The viewer's own pin is marked as theirs.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the teammate Tomás Herrera in Mexico City, when his pin is shown with its label, then it carries the initials "TH", his local time, a weather icon and the temperature.
2. Given a team, when each teammate joins, then they are given a colour from the pin palette that differs from the teammates nearest to them on the globe where the palette allows.
3. Given the viewer's own pin, when it is shown, then it is marked "(you)" wherever a name appears and is distinguishable from other pins by more than colour.
4. Given a teammate who has turned off "Share my local weather", when their pin is shown, then it carries their local time and no weather or temperature.
5. Given a pin on the far side of the globe, when the globe is drawn, then that pin and its label are hidden, and they appear as the globe turns them into view; a pin near the globe's edge is drawn fainter and without its label until it is lifted.
6. Given a name whose first characters are not letters or which is one word, when the pin is shown, then the initials are the first character of up to the first two words, and the pin is never blank.
7. Given a teammate who has turned off both sharing switches (L2-034), when their pin is shown to another teammate, then its label shows their local time alone, and its accessible name gives their name, city and local time and no status or weather.

## L2-012: A pin lifts out when pointed at, focused or selected
**Traces to:** L1-004

On a small globe pins are small. A pin that the pointer is over, that has keyboard focus, or
that is selected rises from the globe's surface, grows, comes in front of its neighbours and
shows its label, so the teammate can be read clearly. When the pointer leaves it settles back.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a pin at rest, when the pointer moves over it, then within 200 ms the pin is drawn larger and raised above the surface, in front of every neighbouring pin, with its label showing the teammate's name, city, local time and weather.
2. Given a lifted pin, when the pointer leaves it and it is not selected, then it settles back to its resting size and place.
3. Given the globe has keyboard focus, when Tab moves to a pin, then that pin lifts exactly as under the pointer, and Enter or Space selects it.
4. Given a touch device, where there is no hover, when a pin is tapped, then it is selected and lifts, and a second tap elsewhere on the globe clears the selection.
5. Given a selected pin, when it stays selected, then it stays lifted and its teammate's details are shown in the panel (L2-023).
6. Given reduced motion is requested, when a pin is pointed at, then it changes to its lifted size and shows its label without the rising animation.
7. Given a pin at rest at any globe size, when its hit area is measured, then it is at least 44 × 44 CSS px.

## L2-013: Pins that are close together stay distinguishable
**Traces to:** L1-004

Teammates in the same city, or in cities close together at the current size and turn of the
globe, are never drawn on top of one another so that one is hidden. Pins that would cover each
other are drawn as one group pin that shows how many teammates it stands for. This holds at
every size of globe and for a team of any size, from two teammates in one city to a team of 500.
Pins are never spread out around a city to keep them apart.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Sofia Bianchi and Luca Ferri, both in Milan, and neither selected, when the globe shows Milan, then one group pin with the count "2" stands at Milan, and its accessible name is "2 teammates close together: Sofia Bianchi (you), Luca Ferri".
2. Given pins that would cover each other at the globe's current size (closer than about 40 px), in one city or in several, when the globe is drawn, then they are drawn as one group pin with their count; a pin with no other pin that close is drawn alone and keeps its label.
3. Given a group pin, when it is selected by pointer, touch or keyboard, then the globe turns to it and the panel lists the teammates it stands for (L2-023); choosing one selects that teammate's pin.
4. Given a selected teammate, however they were selected, when the globe is drawn, then their pin stands alone, lifted and labelled, even where it would otherwise be part of a group pin, and the rest of that group stays a group pin or, if one teammate is left, that teammate's own pin.
5. Given the globe is turned or resized, or a group pin is selected, when the distances between pins change, then the pins are grouped again for the new view, and every teammate on the near side is drawn exactly once, alone or in one group pin.
6. Given a team of 500 teammates, when the Globe screen is shown, then group pins carry their counts (such as "60", "68" and "31"), a group pin's accessible name gives its count and its city, or how many cities it covers ("14 teammates close together, Mexico City", "60 teammates close together, 3 cities"), and the number of pins drawn stays bounded (L2-047).
7. Given teammates in Milan, Berlin, Stockholm and Dublin on a globe 320 px wide, when the globe shows Europe, then each teammate can be selected on their own, from their own pin or through a group pin, and no label overlaps another pin.
8. Given a globe less than 500 px wide, when it is shown, then pins are drawn smaller and a label is shown only for the pin that is lifted or selected.
9. Given a label, when it is placed, then it sits beside its pin, inside the globe's area and clear of other pins.
10. Given a group pin at any globe size, when its hit area is measured, then it is at least 44 × 44 CSS px (L2-012).

## L2-014: Local time
**Traces to:** L1-005

A teammate's local time is worked out in the browser from the time zone of their pin and one
shared clock. It is shown as a 24-hour or 12-hour time as the viewer has chosen, with the day of
the week and the date where a panel shows it ("Monday 27 Oct").

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the clock says 14:05 UTC on 27 October 2025 and a teammate in Mexico City, when their local time is shown on a 24-hour clock, then it reads "08:05"; on a 12-hour clock, "8:05 am".
2. Given a teammate in Bengaluru (UTC+5:30), when the clock says 14:05 UTC, then their local time reads "19:35".
3. Given a screen showing local times, when the minute changes, then every local time on the screen changes together, from one clock, within one second of the minute.
4. Given a daylight-saving change in a teammate's time zone, when the clock passes it, then their local time and offset follow the zone's rule with no reload.
5. Given a teammate in Melbourne when the viewer in Milan is on Monday evening, when the panel is shown, then it shows Melbourne's own day, "Tuesday 28 Oct".
6. Given the backend's response for a pin, when it is inspected, then it carries the pin's time zone as an IANA name and no local time.

## L2-015: Status follows the local hour
**Traces to:** L1-005

A teammate's status is decided only by their local hour. It is never taken from activity,
presence or anything the teammate does.

| Local time | Status |
|---|---|
| 07:00 to 08:59 | Early morning |
| 09:00 to 17:59 | Working hours |
| 18:00 to 22:59 | Evening |
| 23:00 to 06:59 | Probably asleep |

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a local time of 06:59, 07:00, 08:59, 09:00, 17:59, 18:00, 22:59 and 23:00, when the status is worked out, then it is respectively Probably asleep, Early morning, Early morning, Working hours, Working hours, Evening, Evening and Probably asleep.
2. Given a teammate at 08:59 local time shown as "Early morning", when the clock reaches 09:00, then their status changes to "Working hours" with no reload.
3. Given any status, when it is shown, then it is given as its words, never by colour alone.
4. Given a teammate who has turned off "Share whether I’m working or asleep" (L2-034), when another teammate sees them at any hour of their day, then no status is shown for them, they are counted neither as working nor as asleep on the Globe screen (L2-025), and on the Team screen they are listed under All only, not under Working now nor Asleep nor in their counts (L2-028).
5. Given Saturday or Sunday, when the status is worked out, then the same table applies; Mondino keeps no calendar of working days.

## L2-016: Offset from the viewer
**Traces to:** L1-005

A teammate's offset is the difference between their local time and the viewer's, in hours and,
where the zones differ by part of an hour, minutes. It is written with "h" and "m" straight
after the numbers ("7h", "4h 30m"), followed by "behind" or "ahead".

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a viewer in Milan (UTC+1) and a teammate in Mexico City (UTC−6), when the offset is shown, then it reads "7h behind" on their Team row and "7h behind you" on their panel.
2. Given a viewer in Milan (UTC+1) and a teammate in Bengaluru (UTC+5:30), when the offset is shown, then it reads "4h 30m ahead" on their Team row and "4h 30m ahead of you" on their panel; and for a teammate in Melbourne (UTC+11), "10h ahead" and "10h ahead of you".
3. Given a viewer and a teammate whose clocks read the same, when the offset is shown, then it reads "Same time" on their Team row and "same time as you" on their panel, never "0h".
4. Given the viewer's own pin or row, when it is shown, then no offset is given.
5. Given a daylight-saving change on one side only, when it has passed, then the offset shown has changed by one hour.
6. Given the Team screen at 768 px or wider, when the table is shown, then the offset's column is headed "Compared to you"; and under 768 px the offset follows the city on the teammate's row ("Mexico City · 7h behind").

## L2-017: Overlap of working hours
**Traces to:** L1-005

The overlap between the viewer and a teammate is the part of both their working days (09:00 to
18:00 local) that coincides, given on the viewer's own clock, with a bar that draws both working
days, the part they share and the time now across the viewer's day.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a viewer in Milan (UTC+1) and a teammate in Mexico City (UTC−6), when the overlap is shown, then it is 16:00 to 18:00 on the viewer's clock, two hours.
2. Given a viewer in Milan and a teammate in Melbourne (UTC+11), when the overlap is shown, then it reads "None today", says the two share no working hours and gives the teammate's working day on the viewer's clock, 23:00 to 08:00, and the bar shows no shared part.
3. Given a viewer and a teammate in the same time zone, when the overlap is shown, then it is 09:00 to 18:00.
4. Given the viewer has chosen the 12-hour clock, when the overlap is shown, then its times are 12-hour times, a time on the hour without its minutes ("4 pm to 6 pm").
5. Given the overlap bar, when it is read by a screen reader, then the overlap is given as text with its start, end and length.
6. Given the viewer's own pin is selected, when the panel is shown, then no overlap is shown.
7. Given a teammate who has turned off "Share whether I’m working or asleep" (L2-034), when the viewer opens their panel, then no overlap is shown.
8. Given an overlap, when it is shown, then it is headed "Working-hours overlap" and gives its span on the viewer's clock ("16:00–18:00 your time") and in a sentence ("You and Tomás share 2 hours of working hours: 16:00 to 18:00 on your clock.").

## L2-018: Current weather at a teammate's city
**Traces to:** L1-006

Each pin carries the current temperature and one of a fixed set of conditions (clear, partly
cloudy, cloudy, rain, showers, storm, fog, wind) for its city, from Open-Meteo by way of the
backend. Conditions are shown as an icon with its word ("Clear", "Partly cloudy", "Cloudy",
"Rainy", "Showers", "Stormy", "Foggy", "Windy"); at night a clear or partly cloudy sky uses its
night icon.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the weather source reports 13 °C and fog for Milan, when a teammate in Milan is opened, then the panel shows "13°C" and "Foggy in Milan" with the fog icon.
2. Given a teammate whose local time is in "Probably asleep" hours with clear weather, when their weather is shown, then the night form of the icon is used.
3. Given a condition from the source that is not one of the eight, when it is mapped, then it is shown as the nearest of the eight, and never as an empty or broken icon.

## L2-020: The four kinds of moment
**Traces to:** L1-007

A moment is something happening where a teammate lives. It has a kind, a title, a date local to
that place and one line of detail. There are exactly four kinds: a holiday, Formula 1, a local
event and sport. A moment belongs to a teammate when it happens where they live:

| Kind | Belongs to a teammate when |
|---|---|
| Holiday | It is a public holiday in the teammate's country, or in their region where the holiday is regional |
| Formula 1 | A race weekend is held in the teammate's country, or it is a dated happening in that race's own calendar, such as its tickets going on sale |
| Local event | The event is held within 100 km of the teammate's city |
| Sport | A home fixture or major sporting day is held within 100 km of the teammate's city |

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a teammate in Dublin and the October Bank Holiday on 27 October, when their moments are read, then they include a holiday moment titled "October Bank Holiday" dated 27 October.
2. Given a teammate in Milan and a teammate in Austin, when the Italian Grand Prix weekend is in the calendar, then it is a Formula 1 moment for the teammate in Milan and not for the teammate in Austin.
3. Given a teammate in Mississauga and a teammate in Toronto, when the Toronto Raptors have a home game, then it is a sport moment for both, and not for a teammate in Ottawa.
4. Given a regional holiday that applies to Lombardy only, when moments are read, then it belongs to a teammate in Milan and not to a teammate in Rome.
5. Given two teammates in the same city with the same moment, when moments are listed, then the moment appears once for each teammate it belongs to, each naming its teammate.
6. Given a moment's date, when it is compared with "today", then "today" is the date in the moment's own place, so a moment dated 4 November in Melbourne is "today" while it is 4 November in Melbourne.
7. Given a moment from an outside source, when its title and detail are shown, then they are rendered as text and limited to 80 and 160 characters, cut with an ellipsis.
8. Given a teammate in Milan and a teammate in Austin, when the Italian Grand Prix's calendar has its tickets going on sale on 12 November, then "Monza tickets on sale", dated 12 November, is a Formula 1 moment for the teammate in Milan and not for the teammate in Austin.

## L2-043: A teammate sees only their own team and changes only their own pin
**Traces to:** L1-014

concerns, except those that are open to anyone (L2-045): opening an invite link, looking up a
postal code or finding a city while joining or making a team, joining, making a team, asking for

10. Given a sign-in link, when its token is issued, then it carries at least 128 bits of randomness, works once and for 15 minutes, and is kept by the backend only in a form from which the link cannot be rebuilt.
11. Given a teammate's email, when any response about a team, a teammate or a pin is inspected, then no email is in it; an email is never shown or sent to another teammate.
12. Given any teammate of a team, when they ask for the team's invite link or reset it (L2-055), then it is allowed, whoever made the team; and given someone who is not a teammate of that team, then it is refused as in criteria 1 and 2.

## L2-045: What is open to anyone is rate limited, but for making a team
**Traces to:** L1-014

Opening an invite link, looking up a postal code or finding a city while joining, and joining
are open to anyone who holds an invite link. Sign In (asking for a sign-in link and opening one)
and Create Team (its postal code lookup, its search for a city and making the team) are open to
anyone at all. Each is marked as open explicitly and is rate limited, with one exception:
making a team is the one open request with no rate limit (ADR 0016). Create Team's postal code
lookup and its search for a city stay limited.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given one client makes more than 10 postal code lookups in a minute, when the next arrives, then it is refused with 429 and a `Retry-After` header, and the outside source is not called.
2. Given one client opens more than 30 invite links in a minute, when the next arrives, then it is refused with 429, so invite links cannot be guessed at speed.
3. Given one client makes more than 5 join requests in a minute, when the next arrives, then it is refused with 429.
4. Given a postal code lookup or a search for a city on Join, when it is made, then it needs a valid invite link's token; on Create Team, where there is no invite link, the lookup is open to anyone and is held to the same limit as criterion 1.
5. Given a postal code lookup or a join request refused with 429, when it is shown on the Join screen, or a postal code lookup refused with 429 on Create Team, then an alert says "Too many tries from this device." and "You can try again in" the time left ("9:42"), counted down from what the backend gave; the countdown is announced once and not every second, the button is disabled until it reaches zero and then comes back, and what was typed is kept. A refused open of an invite link has nothing typed to keep and shows the state of L2-001.
6. Given a postal code lookup refused with 429 on Profile & Settings, when it is shown, then the message says "That was a lot of tries in a short time. Wait a moment, then try again." (L2-032).
7. Given one client asks for more than 5 sign-in links in 15 minutes, when the next request arrives, then it is refused with 429 and a `Retry-After` header and no mail is sent.
8. Given more than 3 sign-in links have been asked for one email in 15 minutes, from any number of clients, when the next request arrives, then no further mail is sent and the answer is the same as for any other request (L2-043), so nobody's inbox can be flooded and nothing is learned about the email.
9. Given one client opens more than 10 sign-in links in a minute, when the next arrives, then it is refused with 429, so sign-in links cannot be guessed at speed.
10. Given one client has made five teams within an hour, when it asks for another, then the team is made as any other.
11. Given every other endpoint except the health endpoints (L2-051), when it is called without a teammate session, then it is refused; no endpoint is open by omission.

**Not designed yet.** What Sign In shows when a request is refused with 429 is not drawn; Create Team shows Join's countdown for a refused postal code lookup (criterion 5). The limits above are applied by the backend from the start; Sign In's own state for it is not built before its design exists.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given the weather source is failing, when a teammate reads their team, then the response succeeds, every pin has its city, position and time zone, and weather is marked as unavailable rather than given as zero.
2. Given weather is unavailable for a pin, when the pin, panel and Team row are shown, then they show the local time and status; the pin's label and accessible name leave the weather out, the weather's place in the panel says "Weather for Mexico City isn’t available right now. We’ll try again in a few minutes.", and the Team screen says above its list "Weather is unavailable right now. Local times are still right."; nothing is broken or blank.
3. Given the weather source is failing but a cached answer less than 3 hours old exists, when the team is read, then the cached weather is given.
4. Given a calendar source is failing, when moments are read, then moments of the other kinds are still given, with the last cached moments of the failing kind.
5. Given any outside source takes longer than 5 seconds, when the call times out, then the request that needed it completes with what the backend has; no response to a browser waits longer than 6 seconds on an outside source.
6. Given an outside source keeps failing, when calls continue, then the backend stops calling it for a short interval before trying again, and does not queue calls without limit.
7. Given the backend itself cannot be reached, when a screen is open, then it keeps its last data, the clocks keep ticking, and the banner of L2-048 says it could not be refreshed and offers to try again.
8. Given a screen's first read has not answered yet, when the screen is shown, then it shows its loading state: a placeholder in the shape of the content, announced politely with the mock's words ("Loading your team…" on the Globe, "Loading team…" on Team, "Loading moments…", "Loading your pin and settings…").
9. Given a screen's first read fails, when the screen is shown, then it shows its error state with the mock's words and "Try again", and no stale or partial team data: on Globe "The globe didn’t load" and "We couldn’t reach Mondino to fetch your team. Check your connection — nothing you set has been lost."; on Team "The team didn’t load" and "We couldn’t reach Mondino. Check your connection and try again."; on Moments "Moments didn’t load"; on Profile & Settings "Your settings didn’t load".
10. Given the postal code source is failing, when a person is joining or making a team, then they place their pin by picking their city (L2-004), and nobody is kept from joining because the lookup is down.

## L2-050: An outside source learns a place and nothing else
**Traces to:** L1-016

A call to an outside source carries a place (a country and postal code, or a position, or a
country and year) and never a teammate's name, their team or anything that identifies them. The
one exception is the service that sends sign-in links, which has to be given the email address
it sends to, and is given nothing more.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a postal code lookup, when the request to the outside source is inspected, then it carries the country and postal code and no name, team, teammate id, session or client address.
2. Given a weather request, when it is inspected, then it carries the city's position, rounded to two decimal places, and nothing else about the teammate.
3. Given two teammates in the same city, when their weather is needed, then one call is made for the city.
4. Given any screen, when the browser's requests are recorded, then the browser itself calls no outside source (see L2-044).
5. Given a sign-in link is mailed (L2-052), when the request to the service that sends it is inspected, then it carries the email address and the link and no name, team, city, postal code or position.

## L2-051: Telemetry that carries no teammate
**Traces to:** L1-017

The backend emits OpenTelemetry traces, metrics and logs, enough to see its health and the
health of each outside source. None of it carries a name, an email, a postal code, a position, a
city, a token, a query string or a body, and a route is recorded as its template.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a join with the name "Sofia Bianchi" and postal code "20121", when every log, span and metric it produced is searched, then neither value, nor Milan, nor its coordinates, nor the invite link's token appears; and given a sign-in link asked for and opened, then neither the email nor the sign-in link's token appears.
2. Given a request to a team's endpoint, when its span is inspected, then the route is recorded as its template (`/api/teams/{teamId}/pins`), with no id, query string or body.
3. Given calls to the postal code, weather and calendar sources, when metrics are read, then each source has a count of calls, failures and time-outs and a latency histogram, labelled by source and outcome only.
4. Given the backend, when its health endpoints are called, then liveness and readiness are reported without exposing configuration, versions of dependencies or teammate data.
5. Given an unhandled error, when it is logged, then the log carries the error's type and a correlation id that also appears in the ProblemDetails returned, and no request data.
6. Given the frontend, when it is used, then it sends no analytics or telemetry to a third party.

## L2-052: Sign in with an emailed link
**Traces to:** L1-018

Sign In (`/sign-in`) is for a teammate who is already on a team and has come back on a new
browser or device. There is no password: the teammate gives their work email, and Mondino emails
a link that signs them straight in. The link works once, for 15 minutes. A newcomer opens their
team's invite link instead, or starts a team.

**Not designed yet.** Join asks for a name, a country and a postal code and no email (L2-002);
only Create Team collects an email (L2-053). So a teammate who joined by invite link has no email
on record, and how such a teammate signs in again is not designed. Nor is what is shown when an
emailed link has expired or was already used, or while one is being opened: until it is, Sign In
shows its form. None of these is built before its design exists, and no email is asked for on
Join until then. An email on more than one team opens the team it joined or made last, and the
email is plain text in the screen's own words (ADR 0011).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Sign In, when it is shown, then it has the title "Welcome back.", the line "No passwords. We’ll email you a link that signs you straight in.", one field, "Work email", the button "Email me a sign-in link", and a globe with no pins; it has no other field and shows nothing about any team.
2. Given Sign In, when it is shown, then under the form it says "New here?" and "or open the invite link a teammate sent you.", with "Start a team", which leads to Create Team (L2-053).
3. Given text that is not in the form of an email address, when the button is pressed, then no request is sent, the field is marked invalid (`aria-invalid`), the message "That doesn’t look like an email address." is shown under it as an alert, and focus returns to the field; the message goes as soon as the field is edited.
4. Given an email address, when the button is pressed and the request has not been answered, then the button reads "Sending link…" and is disabled.
5. Given the request is answered, when the screen updates, then it reads "Check your inbox" and "We sent a sign-in link to" the email as it was typed, shown as text ("sofia@northwind.co"), and "It works once, for the next 15 minutes."; it is announced to assistive technology and focus moves to the title.
6. Given an email that belongs to no teammate, when a sign-in link is asked for it, then the screen reads "Check your inbox" in exactly the same way, and the response is the same (L2-043); the screen never says whether Mondino knows an email.
7. Given "Check your inbox", when "Use another email" is used, then the form is shown again.
8. Given the backend does not answer, when a sign-in link is asked for, then an alert says "We couldn’t send the link." and "Mondino didn’t answer — try again in a moment.", the button reads "Try again", and the email typed is kept.
9. Given a sign-in link for the email of a teammate, when it is opened for the first time within 15 minutes, then a session is issued for that teammate (L2-043) and the Globe screen of their team is shown; the address shown afterwards carries no token.
10. Given a sign-in link for an email that is on no team, when it is opened, then no session for any team is issued and the screen shows "You need an invite link" naming the email (L2-038).
11. Given a sign-in link that has been opened once, or one more than 15 minutes old, when it is opened, then it signs no one in and issues no session.
12. Given requests for sign-in links and openings of them, when they arrive too often, then they are refused or answered without sending mail as L2-045 sets out.
13. Given a sign-in link is mailed, when the mail is sent, then the service that sends it is given the email address and the link and nothing else: no name, team, city or postal code (L2-050); and the link's token appears in no log, metric or span (L2-051).

## L2-053: Make a team
**Traces to:** L1-018

Create Team (`/new`), "Start a team.", lets anyone make a team: they name it, place their own
pin, and get the invite link for everyone else. The person who makes a team is its first
teammate and a teammate like any other; Mondino has no roles. The work email given here is
where sign-in links are sent (L2-052) and is never shown to a teammate.

An email that already belongs to a teammate of another team makes a team like any other email:
Create Team answers the same way whether or not Mondino knows it (L2-043), and which team a
sign-in link for it opens is L2-052's. A team's name, like a teammate's, has at most 60
characters (ADR 0009).

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given Create Team, when it is shown, then it has the title "Start a team.", the line "Name it, place your own pin, and you’ll get a link to invite everyone else.", the fields "Team name", "Your name", "Work email" (with the help "We’ll send sign-in links here."), "Country" and "Postal code", the text "Only your city is shown. Your postal code stays private.", the button "Create team", and a globe with no pins.
2. Given the Country field, when it is opened, then it lists the same supported countries as Join (L2-002).
3. Given a postal code, when it is looked up on Create Team, then it is looked up as on Join, with the same messages when it cannot be found or the lookup is down (L2-003, L2-004), and the pin is placed at the city (L2-006).
4. Given the form with no team name, when the button is pressed, then no team is made, the field is marked invalid (`aria-invalid`), the message "Give your team a name — teammates see it when they join." is shown under it, and focus moves to the first field that is wrong.
5. Given a missing name, email or postal code, or an email that is not in the form of an email address, when the button is pressed, then each is said in the same way, under its own field, and no team is made: "Add your name so teammates know whose pin this is.", "Add your work email — we’ll send sign-in links there.", "That doesn’t look like an email address." and "Enter a postal code."
6. Given a valid form with a found postal code, when "Create team" is used and the request has not been answered, then the button reads "Creating your team…" and is disabled.
7. Given the request succeeds, when it is answered, then a team with that name exists whose one teammate is its maker, with a pin at their city; a session is issued to them (L2-043); and the team has an invite link.
8. Given the team has been made, when the screen updates, then it reads "Northwind is on the map" (announced to assistive technology), "Now bring everyone in." and "Send this link to your team. Each person places their own pin with a postal code.", shows the invite link with "Copy link", and offers "Go to the globe"; focus moves to the title.
9. Given the team has been made, when the screen updates, then the globe turns to the maker's city and holds, and their new pin, labelled "You · Milan", pulses (not under reduced motion).
10. Given "Copy link", when it is used and the copy succeeds, then the link is on the clipboard and the button reads "Copied"; and given the browser refuses the copy, then the link is selected and the same toast as in the invite dialog is shown (L2-054).
11. Given "Go to the globe", when it is used, then the Globe screen of the new team is shown: a team of one (L2-025).
12. Given the request fails, when the failure is reported, then an alert says "We couldn’t create Northwind." and "Nothing was saved — try again.", naming the team as text, the button reads "Try again", what was typed is kept, and no team, teammate or pin has been stored.
13. Given the maker double-clicks "Create team", when both requests arrive, then exactly one team is made.
14. Given Create Team, when it is shown, then under the form it says "Already have a team?" with "Sign in", which leads to Sign In (L2-052).
15. Given a team's name and its maker's name, when they are stored and shown, then they are validated by the backend and rendered as text (L2-044).
16. Given Create Team's postal code lookup, when it is made too often from one client and refused with 429 (L2-045), then the screen shows the countdown of L2-045 criterion 5 with what was typed kept; only the lookup can be refused for coming too often, and the request to make a team never is (L2-045 criterion 10).
17. Given an email that is already a teammate's email on another team, when a team is made with it, then the team is made as with any other email and the answer is the same, so it says nothing of which emails Mondino knows (L2-043).

## L2-055: Reset the invite link
**Traces to:** L1-018

Any teammate can reset the team's invite link from the invite dialog ("Reset link"). The old
link stops working and a new one takes its place. Nothing else changes: the teammates already
on the team keep their pins and stay signed in.

**Not designed yet.** What "Reset link" asks before it acts, and what the dialog says once the
link has been reset, are not drawn. Resetting is not built before its design exists; the
criteria below are what it must then hold to.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a team's invite link has been reset, when a person opens the old link, then the Join screen shows "This invite link no longer works" and no team name, no count and no pins (L2-001).
2. Given a team's invite link has been reset, when a join request or a postal code lookup arrives with the old link's token, then it is refused, and no pin is made.
3. Given a reset, when it is done, then the team has a new invite link with a new token (L2-043), and the invite dialog shows the new link in place of the old one.
4. Given a reset, when it is done, then every teammate already on the team keeps their pin, their settings and their session.
5. Given any teammate of the team, when they reset the link, then it is allowed; and given a request to reset from someone who is not a teammate of that team, then it is refused with 401 or 404 and the link is unchanged (L2-043).
6. Given a person on the Join screen of a link that is reset before they join, when they choose "Join", then no pin is made and the screen shows "This invite link no longer works".

## L2-057: What a team stores is kept
**Traces to:** L1-018

A deployed Mondino keeps what a team stores (the team and its invite link, its teammates and
their pins, and its sign-in links) in a database, so a restart or a new version loses none of
it and a teammate who comes back finds their team as they left it. A new version's changes to
the database are applied by the deployment, before that version serves a request.

**Acceptance Criteria:**
1. Given a team made on the deployed Mondino, when the backend restarts, then the team's invite link still opens the same team, with the same teammates and pins.
2. Given a team on the deployed Mondino, when a new version is deployed, then the team, its teammates, their pins and its sign-in links are as they were.
3. Given a new version that changes the database, when it is deployed, then the changes are applied before the new version serves a request; and given the changes cannot be applied, then the deployment stops and the version that was running goes on serving.
4. Given a deployed backend that cannot reach its database, when its readiness is asked (L2-051), then it reports that it is not ready, and a deployment that ends in that state fails.

These criteria are about a deployed system, so they are proven on it by the deployment and its
checks. An acceptance test runs on no real database (`AGENTS.md`); readiness itself is proven
by acceptance tests under L2-051.
