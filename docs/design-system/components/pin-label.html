Pin label

The chip beside a pin: the teammate’s local time, weather and temperature.

Overview

At rest the label is a small white chip with the local time, a weather icon and the temperature. When its pin lifts, the label opens into the teammate read clearly: name and city, then clock and sky, then any moment this week.

Anatomy

Its parts, in reading order:

  1. Name and cityGabarito, 14 px
  2. Local timeSemibold, tabular figures
  3. Weather and temperatureIcon, degrees, and the word when lifted
  4. Moment (optional)In sun: title and when

Responsive behaviour

A label sits beside its pin on whichever side is clear of other pins and inside the globe: right, left, below or above. A group pin has no label; its count is its label, and the panel names who is in it. In a large team only a pin that stands alone keeps a label. On a globe under 500 px wide only the lifted label is shown; it sits under its pin and wraps so it stays inside the globe.

Theming

It reads semantic tokens only, so light and dark both come from tokens.css. To re-theme it, override the tokens listed under Tokens at :root or in a [data-theme] block; never write a colour in the component.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Decorative: aria-hidden="true". Everything it shows is in its pin’s accessible name.

Keyboard

It is not focusable and takes no keys; it is read in place by a screen reader.

Focus

Not focusable; it never takes pointer events.

Labelling

The weather is a word in the pin’s name as well as an icon here. With two or more moments this week the pin’s name lists them all; the lifted label gives the nearest.

Contrast

At rest --color-pin-label-fg on --color-pin-label-bg
Lifted --palette-paper on --palette-ink
Moment line --palette-sun on --palette-ink

Motion

Appears with its pin’s lift. No motion of its own.

Touch

Not a control.

Content

Time first, then weather, then temperature in whole degrees with no unit letter. The viewer’s clock and unit apply. When weather is unavailable or not shared, the label is the time alone.
